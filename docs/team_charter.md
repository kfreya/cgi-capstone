# Team_charter

**Project:** CGI Capacity Analyzer Dashboard\
**Partner:** CGI\
**Team:** Freya, Kian, Lyken, Yixiao

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Roles

We follow a Scrum workflow. All members are the development team. Decisions are made democratically as a group. Freya will act as Scrum Master for the sole purpose of keeping things on track; she does not make final decisions.

There is no dedicated Product Owner on our side; the client (CGI) represents that role. We bring open questions to our mentor or CGI contact as needed.

## Sprints

We work in 1-week sprints. Each sprint starts on Monday and ends on Friday.

-   **Monday Meeting (12:00–12:30 ET, mandatory):** The team reviews what was done the previous week, discusses updates, and plans tasks for the new sprint. Each task goes on the sprint board (GitHub Issues). This also serves as the sprint review and planning ceremony.
-   **Daily Standup (12:00–12:15 ET, not mandatory):** Held every day (Tuesday - Friday). A time to give quick updates and request assistance from the CGI side. Each member covers what they did, what they are doing next, and any blockers.
-   **Sprint Retrospective (end of sprint):** Short discussion on what went well, what did not, what to change next sprint. Kept to 20–30 minutes.

## Backlog

We maintain a shared task board (GitHub Issues + Milestones or equivalent). All tasks are written as clear, small units of work before a sprint starts. During planning, tasks are pulled into the sprint and assigned. Nothing is worked on outside the sprint board; if something comes up, it gets added to the backlog first.

## Work Distribution

Tasks are picked up from the sprint board. We aim for roughly equal contribution across the project, not necessarily within every sprint. If someone finishes early, they pick up the next highest-priority backlog item or help a teammate.

## Definition of Done

A task is considered done when its output is complete, reviewed, and ready to be used by the team.

For code-related tasks, a task is done when:

-   The code works and has been manually tested.
-   At least one other team member has reviewed it.
-   It is merged to `main` through the agreed GitHub workflow without breaking existing functionality.
-   Any relevant documentation, comments, or usage notes are updated.

For non-code tasks, such as analysis, documentation, meeting notes, project planning, or presentation work, a task is done when:

-   The expected output is complete and saved in the agreed project location.
-   At least one other team member has reviewed it for accuracy, clarity, and completeness.
-   Any follow-up action items or unresolved questions are clearly noted.

No task is called "done" just because the first draft or initial code is written.

## Expected Work Hours

No fixed hours. We are flexible around classes and personal schedules. The expectation is to make meaningful progress each sprint and communicate in advance if something will be delayed. Respond to messages within 24 hours on weekdays.

## Availability

Tell the team at least 48 hours in advance if you will be unavailable or need to miss a meeting. If an emergency comes up, let someone know as soon as possible.

## Quality of Work

-   Code is reviewed by at least one other person before merging to main.
-   All substantive changes should go through a pull request before being merged into main.
-   Everyone is responsible for their own part being correct, not just done.
-   If something is not clear, ask early rather than guessing.

## Code of Conduct

-   Be direct. Say what you actually think.
-   Show up to meetings on time or give advance notice.
-   No ghosting; if you are falling behind or stuck, say so early.
-   Critique the work, not the person.
-   Everyone's input is heard before a decision is made.

## Disagreements

Discuss it in the sprint team meeting. Everyone states their view, then the group decides by consensus. If we are stuck, we bring it to our mentor.

## If Someone Is Not Pulling Their Weight

If a contribution concern arises, we will address it early and respectfully rather than waiting until the end of a sprint. The first step is to speak with the person one-on-one to understand whether they are blocked, overloaded, or need support.

If the issue continues, the team will discuss it during the sprint retrospective and agree on concrete next steps, such as redistributing tasks, clarifying expectations, or setting smaller check-in milestones.

We will try to resolve contribution issues internally first. If the team cannot resolve the issue or if it begins to affect the project timeline or team fairness, we will ask our mentor or course staff for guidance.
